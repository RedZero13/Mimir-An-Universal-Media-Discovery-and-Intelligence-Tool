# Mimir: features

Mimir is a free catalogue and discovery engine for films, TV series, books and manga, games, music and YouTube,
built by Terratales Media Entertainment. Live at [mimir.terratalesmedia.world](https://mimir.terratalesmedia.world/).

The catalogue holds about 7,650 works: 3,212 films, 1,439 TV series, 701 books and manga, 1,263 games, 852 albums,
50 YouTube channels and 139 videos, from 99 countries in 68 original languages. The original 681 are fully classified
against a controlled vocabulary of 372 terms (233 of them subgenres); the rest were imported from Wikidata with their
facts and genres and are being classified in batches (see [CATALOGUE-EXPANSION.md](CATALOGUE-EXPANSION.md)).

## Find something

- **Ask Mimir in your own words.** Describe a mood, an idea, a favourite, things to avoid or how long you have
  ("I want to die laughing watching a show", "a book like Outer Wilds", "under two hours, no violence"). Mimir
  shows how it read the request, then returns a few picks, each with the reasons it fits.
- **Every pick explains itself.** Reasons come from the catalogue and say whether each is a recorded fact or an
  editorial reading.
- **Cross-medium.** "Something like Blade Runner but in a different medium" works; so does any medium on its own.
- **Decide for me.** Three taps, one pick, with its reasons.
- **For you.** Rate a few works you know (loved, liked, disliked) and Mimir builds a taste profile in your browser
  and recommends from it, naming the work each pick resembles. Controls steer it: hidden gems or well-known
  works, how long, one pick per series, and familiar picks or a stretch.
- **Experience DNA.** Every work page shows a fingerprint of its moods, themes, pacing, complexity, storytelling,
  world and style. Keep the qualities you want and drop the rest, and Mimir finds works with that mix, in any medium.
- **Help centre (#help).** A search box over 20 guides in six topics (getting started, finding something, your
  library, taste and picks, days and community, privacy and your data), ten frequently asked questions, and the
  walkthrough video. Guides open at #help-<name>; the search filters guides and questions as you type.
- **Swipe to teach Mimir.** A deck of cards, one work at a time: right to like, up to love, left for not for me,
  down to skip what you haven't seen (arrow keys, buttons or a drag; Z undoes). Each swipe is a rating. The first 14
  cards cover as many genres, moods and media as possible; after that the deck narrows towards what you liked, and
  once there is enough signal Mimir offers to take you to your picks (and asks again after another 15 swipes).
- **Don't have time to browse?** Mood cards (Comfort, Laugh out loud, Edge of the seat, After dark, Mind games …).
  Left tries another mood; right spins that mood's posters like a slot machine, lands on one title and opens it after
  a three-second countdown (Enter opens now, right spins again). The moods you spin for nudge your picks.
- **Craft.** Click or drag pieces onto the table, then drop one on another or select two and combine: works, moods, genres and mediums blend into a new
  hybrid ("Comforting science fiction"), and Mimir finds the works that carry the most of it, in any medium or one you
  choose. Blends are kept and can be combined again.
- **Easter eggs.** Some well-known covers react when you click them (a burst of spider-sense, falling code, a crawl
  …). Finding them earns badges; the count is shown without saying which.
- **Mood mixer.** Combine moods and things to avoid ("melancholic but comforting, no bleak") with a medium and a
  time limit, then send it to Ask. Decide for me also takes up to three moods at once.
- **Your taste profile.** See what you lean towards and away from, ask for more or less of any quality, say
  "never" to a quality or a parents' guide item, save contexts (winding down, learning, deep immersion, with
  friends, or your own), and see how your taste has changed over time.
- **Why each pick fits.** A "Why?" on every For you pick shows each shared quality, how strongly it applies to the
  pick and to the work you rated, whether it's a recorded fact or a reading, and its confidence.
- **Compare any two works** side by side: length, scores, popularity, age ratings, parents' guide, and the moods,
  themes and style they share or don't.
- **Days of the year.** A calendar of 50 international and widely observed days (World Laughter Day, International
  Music Day, International Podcast Day, World Mental Health Day, International Jazz Day, World Television Day …),
  dates from the UN and UNESCO lists. Each day has two sets of picks: *something new*, chosen from review scores
  across platforms and leaning towards the lesser known, with a different handful each time and each year; and
  *picked for you*, from your own taste profile. Without a profile, you react to a few well-known fits and the
  section fills in. Every rating feeds the profile used across Mimir. Days of remembrance are framed gently.
- **Plan my time.** Pick an evening, a weekend, a week off or a month; Mimir fills it from what you've started and
  planned first, then from picks for you, day by day, and shows the next work in every series you've started.
- **Pick together.** Add each person, a few things they love and what they'd rather avoid; Mimir finds picks that
  suit everyone and says where the compromise is.
- **By the time you have.** Quick bite, one sitting, an evening or two, a weekend, a season, a long haul: from
  runtime, main-story playtime, or reading time from page count.
- **Search** titles, people and studios from the menu bar, scoped to a medium.

## Browse

- **Directories per medium** (films, TV, books, games, music, YouTube), each showing only that medium, with
  filters for tag, decade, language, country, length and parents' guide category.
- **36 shelves** with wordplay names and a stated rule: Diamonds in the Rough, Nepo Babies, Laugh Riot, Drama Mamas,
  Fright Club, Binge-Worthy, Seoul Searching, Desi Delights, Page Turners, Side Quests, World Builders and more.
  Each has an About panel with its description, its classification rule and its year span.
- **Subgenres for every genre** in every medium (slasher, folk horror, cosy mystery, soulslike, Sufi rock…), grouped
  under their parent on each shelf.
- **Industry filter** for film and TV: Hollywood, India, Japan, Korea, China, France, the UK and more.
- **Top rated** per medium, from a single 0–100 critics' average across the published scores Mimir holds.
- **Sagas and series:** one page per IP (James Bond, Middle-earth, the Marvel Cinematic Universe, the Wizarding
  World, Star Wars …) with a row each for its films, shows, books and games, in order, the time the whole run takes,
  and links to the series inside it. Work pages show every franchise and series a work belongs to.
- **Lead cast** on films and series, from Wikidata, in billing order where recorded; search finds works by actor or
  by franchise.
- **Tag pages** and **people and studio pages** with recurring qualities.
- **Social calendar:** seasonal programmes with a pick for each day (Spooktober, Noirvember, Nonfiction November…).
- **Music:** mood rings and a remix rack.
- **YouTube:** creators worth following, what they make and their best-known videos, with video thumbnails as
  covers, grouped as comedy, food, gaming and horror, VFX and film, mysteries and music.
- **Audiobooks:** books grouped by listening time. 88 books show their real Audible recording (narrator, length,
  release year); public-domain books link their free LibriVox recording.
- **Food:** cooking competitions, food travel, food documentaries, taste tests and food dramas across film, TV, books
  and games.

## Every work page

- Facts with their source (Wikidata, Open Library, MusicBrainz, the publisher) and what the seed said where they differ.
- Tags by dimension (genre, theme, mood, pacing, complexity, narrative, world, style), with strength and basis.
- **Parents' guide** for every medium: official ratings plus per-category severity and notes, telling "none" apart
  from "not assessed".
- Connected stories: adaptations, sequels, spin-offs, the same universe.
- **Where to watch, listen, read or play** for your region (US, UK, India, Canada, Australia), with your preferred
  apps first.
- Pinned section tabs that follow you down the page.

## Your library

- Statuses, scores, progress (episodes, pages, hours for audiobooks, playtime) and a diary.
- **Lists:** playlists, reading lists, watch lists and bookmarks, each with a share link and social sharing.
- **Custom trackers** for any goal you set.
- **Activity rings** (watch, read, play, listen) with daily goals, and **about 80 badges** for milestones
  (1st, 5th, 10th, 50th…), visit streaks, days you've opened Mimir, and how often you share works, lists and pages.
  Each share button shows how many times you've shared that link.
- **Journal**, **stats** and a **year in review**.
- **Reset my recommendations:** in your profile (and under Import & export), one confirmed click deletes your library,
  ratings, "not for me" marks, feedback on picks and taste edits so recommendations start again from nothing.
  Lists, journal, trackers and rings are kept, and a signed-in account's synced library is cleared too.
- **Import and export** your whole library as a file.
- Everything lives in your browser unless you sign in.

## Accounts that cannot read your data
- **A recovery key is your account.** No email or password. The browser encrypts your library, lists, ratings, journal and
  settings before they leave the device; the server stores only ciphertext, so Mimir cannot read it.
- **Sync across devices** with per-item merging, export your data as a file, change your key, sign out (optionally wiping
  this device) or delete everything on the server in one step. A lost key cannot be recovered.
- Needs the Mimir Vault plugin switched on: see [ACCOUNTS.md](ACCOUNTS.md). Sign in with Google, Apple and Meta is planned.
- **Privacy you can change:** analytics is off until you allow it (a plain consent panel, honouring Global Privacy Control),
  nothing you search for, rate or save is ever collected, and the privacy notice, cookie policy, terms, data requests and
  security pages sit in every footer.

## Community

- Accounts with profiles, privacy settings and account deletion.
- Reviews with a community score, likes and comments; follow people and see their activity.
- Published lists and social-calendar challenges with leaders.
- **Watch-alongs:** start one on any film or series, leave comments at timestamps that stay hidden until others
  reach that moment, share the link, and see live watch-alongs in Community.
- Reports for reviews, comments, users and lists.

## Built to be fast, findable and honest

- The home page is about 120 KB and paints its headline with the first bytes; data and code load after it and are
  cached for repeat visits and offline use (installable as an app on Android, iOS, Mac and Windows).
- Plain pages for every work, medium and shelf, with structured data and a sitemap, so search engines can index
  the catalogue; works still awaiting a summary are kept out of the index until they have one.
- Dark mode by default with a light mode toggle, keyboard navigation, reduced-motion support and WCAG AA contrast (checked with axe in both themes).
- Labels everywhere say what is verified and what is an AI-assisted reading awaiting review.

## Under the hood

- Python (FastAPI, PostgreSQL) API and a Next.js web app; the public site runs the same discovery engine in the
  browser, kept identical to the Python by parity tests on every query.
- No LLM and no API keys: requests are parsed into inspectable constraints, ranked, and explained from evidence.
- Open data only, each source in a licensing register ([`SOURCES.md`](SOURCES.md)).
